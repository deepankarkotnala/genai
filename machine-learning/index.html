Machine Learning Interview Preparation

A chronological course through the algorithms, mathematics, evaluation discipline and production concerns that classical machine learning interviews actually test.

25 modules · study in order

Learn the reasoning, not the recipe

Because code generation is now trivial, 2026 interviews stopped rewarding syntax recall and moved to reasoning: why this model, why this loss, why this metric, why this split, and what breaks in production. Nearly every question in this bank is a “why” question, and every module ends with the questions and model answers for its topic.

196 interview questions25 modules · 6 parts≈180 h full track
🎯
Your interview goal

Be able to derive a loss from a probability model, choose a metric from a business cost, place any hyperparameter on the bias–variance dial, explain what each boosting tree actually fits, and spot data leakage before it reaches production.

🧭
Representative questions, not sourced claims

These questions reflect the concepts, phrasings and follow-up patterns that dominate ML interview loops in the 2025–2026 hiring cycle. They are not claims that a named company asks a specific question. What transfers between companies is the concept being tested and the shape of the follow-up, never the wording.

How to work through this curriculum

Study the modules in order — the dependency chain is real, and each module assumes the ones before it. For every module: read the concept checklist, re-explain each intuition out loud in under 60 seconds, write the formulas from memory on paper, complete the hands-on drill, then attempt every interview question before opening the answer.

Sprint · 3 weeks4–5 h/day. Modules 00–18 plus mocks. Algorithms and metrics, depth skipped.
Standard · 8 weeks2 h/day. Every module, every drill. The recommended track.
Deep · 14 weeks2 h/day. Every module plus from-scratch derivations and two portfolio projects.

A better practice loop

1

Answer closed-book

Give yourself 60–90 seconds before opening the answer. Lead with the principle, then the mechanism, then the consequence.

2

Compress to 30 seconds

Most questions are answered in under a minute, then deepened by follow-ups. Practise the short version, not the essay.

3

Say the trade-off

State what you would choose, why, and what constraint would make you choose differently. That sentence is what gets scored.
