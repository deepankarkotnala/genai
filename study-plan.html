Your minimum study commitment

Target 2–4 focused hours each weekday and 8 focused hours across the weekend. That produces roughly 18–28 hours per week. At that pace the path below takes approximately 7–15 weeks, including the Python rail. Short on time? Read Level 1 of every topic first and come back for Levels 2 and 3 - the order still holds.

How to use this plan

This is a single, sequential learning path. Its goal is that you understand each topic well enough to explain it from memory weeks later. Every page on it earns its place, and the order is fixed so nothing asks you to know something you have not studied yet. Open step 01, finish it, open step 02. On the long topic pages, read Level 1 first - about 30 minutes - and come back for Levels 2 and 3 when the rest of the phase is done.

Every topic follows the same loop: read Level 1, check yourself with its recall questions, review its cards 1, 3, 7 and 21 days later, then practise with the questions and scenarios that follow it on the path.

Phase 1 - LLM foundations

Steps 01–04 · 21–31 hours. Everything downstream assumes you can explain next-token prediction, attention and a forward pass without notes. Step 02 lives in the Interview Questions folder, but it is a teaching page - do not skip it.

🧠Phase 1 reading order
  1. Foundations of LLMs 2–3 hTokens, next-token prediction, decoding, context windows and prompting. The vocabulary every later page uses.
  2. Neural Networks for AI Engineers 12–18 hNeuron intuition, forward pass, loss, backpropagation and optimisation. Gives you the gradient idea step 03 leans on.
  3. Transformers 2.5–4 hAttention as score, softmax, blend - worked by hand - then Q/K/V, masking, positional information and the KV cache. Level 1 first.
  4. Question bank - Foundations & prompting 4–6 hConvert phase 1 into spoken answers. Do this before moving on, not at the end of the route.
✓
Phase 1 checkpoint

Explain attention as score, softmax, blend in under two minutes, then draw one transformer block from memory and say why attention cost grows with the square of the length. Answer every question in step 04 aloud without reading.

Phase 2 - Retrieval and RAG

Steps 05–09 · 16–25 hours. RAG is the single most likely subject of a technical round. It lives on exactly one page in this portal - read it in order rather than skipping to reranking. Step 08 is your first technology drill; the other drills appear in phases 3, 4 and 6, each right after the material it tests.

🔎Phase 2 reading order
  1. Embeddings 2–3 hVector intuition, similarity, model choice and failure cases. Build the intuition before the database that stores them.
  2. Vector Databases 2–3 hApproximate search, HNSW, filtering and the recall/latency trade-off.
  3. RAG, End-to-End 3–5 hOne question traced through the whole pipeline first, then chunking, hybrid search, reranking, query rewriting and evaluation.
  4. Drill - RAG 4–6 hTimed technical drill on what you just read. Take it before the question bank, while the gaps are still visible.
  5. Question bank - Embeddings & RAG 5–8 hCloses phase 2. You should be able to defend a chunking strategy against a challenge.
✓
Phase 2 checkpoint

Draw the indexing and query pipelines from memory. Explain when hybrid search beats dense-only, what a reranker costs you in latency, and how you would tell whether retrieval or generation caused a bad answer.

Phase 3 - Agents

Steps 10–14 · 15–24 hours. When an LLM stops talking and starts doing. These pages give you the agent loop, the protocol that connects tools to it, and what an agent remembers - the vocabulary every framework in phase 4 assumes.

⚙Phase 3 reading order
  1. Agentic AI 3–5 hThe agent loop, tool calling, planning, stopping conditions, reliability, human approval and prompt injection - and when not to use an agent.
  2. Model Context Protocol 2–3 hHost, client and server roles; tools, resources and prompts; security; and how A2A differs from MCP.
  3. Memory 15–30 minShort-term versus long-term memory, summarisation and retrieved memory. A short page - only what the interview needs.
  4. Drill - MCP 4–6 hTimed drill on step 11, while the protocol is fresh.
  5. Question bank - Agents, LangGraph & MCP 6–9 hCovers this phase. Its LangGraph questions are a preview - come back to them after step 17.
✓
Phase 3 checkpoint

Draw an agent loop on a whiteboard with no framework: the prompt, the tool schema, the parse step, the guard, the stop condition. Name three ways it fails in production, and explain in one minute what problem MCP solves.

Phase 4 - Frameworks

Steps 15–18 · 10–15 hours. You now know what an agent is, so these pages are about what each framework buys you and what it costs.

🕸Phase 4 reading order
  1. LangChain 2–3 hRunnables, LCEL, retrievers, structured output and how to debug through the abstraction.
  2. Drill - LangChain 4–6 hTimed drill immediately after the topic, while the API is fresh.
  3. LangGraph 2–3 hState, nodes, reducers, routing, checkpointing and human review. The agent loop from step 10, made explicit as a graph.
  4. Multi-Agent Systems 1.5–3 hSupervisor, handoff, shared state and coordination failure modes - and when one agent is the better answer.
✓
Phase 4 checkpoint

Given a requirement, choose between a plain loop, LangChain, LangGraph and a multi-agent design - and justify the choice against latency, debuggability and failure blast radius. Explain what a checkpoint gives you that a retry does not.

Phase 5 - Production, safety and evaluation

Steps 19–25 · 17–28 hours. This is the phase that separates a candidate who has seen a demo from one who can run a system. The production topic sets the frame; the three short topics then cover the parts interviewers probe hardest.

📈Phase 5 reading order
  1. Production GenAI 2–3 hLatency, cost, caching, rate limits and deployment. The frame for everything below.
  2. Guardrails 15–30 minInput and output checks, prompt injection, PII and human approval - layered defence in one short page.
  3. LLM Evals 15–30 minGolden sets, RAG metrics, LLM-as-judge and its biases, and regression tests.
  4. LLMOps & Observability 15–30 minTracing, prompt versioning, cost and latency monitoring, and feedback loops in production.
  5. Question bank - Evaluation & LLMOps 5–8 hCovers steps 21 and 22.
  6. Question bank - Production, latency & cost 5–8 hCovers step 19 and the numeric reasoning interviewers push on.
  7. Question bank - Security & responsible AI 4–7 hCovers step 20 and the safety parts of step 10.
✓
Phase 5 checkpoint

Take a described production incident - quality regression after a model swap - and talk through detection, diagnosis, rollback and the gate you would add. Name the eval metrics you would track and give rough numbers for p95 latency and cost per request.

Phase 6 - Backend, APIs and data

Steps 26–30 · 22–34 hours. AI Engineer roles are backend roles. This short phase covers the engineering around the model that backend screens ask about.

🔌Phase 6 reading order
  1. Drill - Sync vs Async Python 4–6 hConcurrency questions appear in almost every AI backend screen. Pairs with P10 on the Python rail.
  2. Drill - FastAPI 4–6 hThe default serving layer for LLM applications.
  3. Drill - WebSockets 3–5 hStreaming responses and long-lived agent sessions.
  4. SQL for GenAI roles 5–8 hText-to-SQL, metadata filtering and the queries behind retrieval. Needed for scenario 3 in phase 7.
  5. Question bank - Python, backend & cloud 6–9 hCloses phase 6 and overlaps the Python rail - a good place to check the rail is keeping up.
✓
Phase 6 checkpoint

Explain, aloud and with a sketch, how you would serve a streaming model response from a FastAPI endpoint over WebSockets. Say when async helps and when it does nothing.

Phase 7 - System design

Steps 31–40 · 24–36 hours. The design round is where everything above gets tested together. Read the answer framework first - the eight scenarios are much less useful without it - then do each scenario timed and out loud.

🏗Phase 7 reading order
  1. Answer framework 2–3 hThe structure you will use for all eight scenarios and the system-design round. Learn it before drilling.
  2. Scenario 1 - Enterprise knowledge assistant 2–3 hTimed. Phase 2 retrieval at organisational scale, with permissions and citations.
  3. Scenario 2 - Customer support agent 2–3 hTimed. The agent loop from phase 3 with tools, hand-off to a human and PII.
  4. Scenario 3 - Secure text-to-SQL 2–3 hTimed. Needs step 29 and the guardrails from step 20.
  5. Scenario 4 - ATS recruiter copilot 2–3 hTimed. Fairness and responsible-AI constraints on top of retrieval.
  6. Scenario 5 - Multilingual voice assistant 2–3 hTimed. Latency budgets become the dominant constraint.
  7. Scenario 6 - Invoice document workflow 2–3 hTimed. Extraction accuracy and human approval for irreversible actions, from step 10.
  8. Scenario 7 - High-scale shopping assistant 2–3 hTimed. Cost per request at volume - the phase 5 numbers matter here.
  9. Scenario 8 - Financial research copilot 2–3 hTimed. Auditability, citations and regulated-domain constraints.
  10. GenAI system design 6–9 hGeneralises the eight scenarios into a repeatable round performance.
✓
Phase 7 checkpoint

Deliver any of the eight scenarios cold, in 30 minutes, with a diagram and named trade-offs: retrieval, safety, evaluation, latency and cost.

Phase 8 - Interview conversion

Steps 41–42 · 13–20 hours. Knowledge you cannot deliver under time pressure does not count. Everything here is spoken, timed and out loud.

🎤Phase 8 reading order
  1. Project & behavioural 5–8 hTurn your own project - from work or study - into a clear story you can defend for forty minutes.
  2. Mock interview rounds 8–12 hFull timed rounds. The last step - treat a weak round as a pointer back to a phase, not a verdict.
✓
Phase 8 checkpoint

Tell your project story in five minutes, answer the follow-ups without notes, and finish two consecutive mock rounds without needing to look anything up.

Three rules for using the plan

→

Do not reorder the spine

Each step's why line names what it depends on. Skipping ahead is the most common reason a page feels hard - the missing piece is almost always two or three steps back.

⌁

One page per topic

Every topic has a single home in this portal. If you feel you are re-reading something, you have wandered off the sequence - return to your step number.

⌨

Count active work

Hours count when you retrieve from memory, draw systems or answer aloud - not when a page is merely open. A checkpoint you cannot pass means the hours have not been spent.
