12 · Interview cheat-sheet

Q. What is LLMOps and how is it different from MLOps?

LLMOps is MLOps for apps built on foundation models. You usually don't train the model, so the focus moves from training pipelines and retraining-on-drift to prompts/config versioning, retrieval, evaluation, observability, guardrails, and token cost/latency of the app around a hosted model.

Q. Walk me through the LLMOps lifecycle.

Experiment with prompts/models/config → evaluate on a golden set → deploy with guardrails → observe via traces (cost, latency, quality) → feed real failures back into the next experiment. It's a continuous loop.

Q. Why version prompts and pin models?

The prompt is the behavior-defining code, so changes need history and rollback. Models change under you - a silent provider update can shift outputs - so you pin the version and re-run evals before adopting a new one.

Q. How do you reduce LLM cost without wrecking quality?

Baseline first, then apply one lever at a time: trim/compress context, cap output length, right-size the model per task, cache, and cut unnecessary agent steps - stopping at the threshold where quality starts to degrade.

Q. What's the difference between offline and online evaluation?

Offline runs on a curated golden set before deploy and gates releases (CI for prompts). Online runs on live traffic - trace scores and user feedback - to catch quality drift and surface new failure cases in production.

Q. What does a trace contain and why does it matter?

One request end-to-end: each step, tool call, prompt, response, tokens and latency. It's the foundation for debugging, cost/latency metrics, and attaching quality scores - you can't operate or improve what you can't see.
